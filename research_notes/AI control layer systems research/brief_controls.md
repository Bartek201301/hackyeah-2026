# Partner brief: obligations, control flow, and corrections to team notes

## What does the four-page brief explicitly require, and what is guidance or team interpretation?

### Takeaway

The deliverable is a working, integrable control layer with one central policy source, hybrid controls, budgets, historical-attack mitigation, reporting/audit, and runnable positive and negative tests. The document gives teams broad choice of implementation, agent, model, and demo domain; the current analyst/outbox/Next.js/Supabase/Laya design is a team proposal, not a partner requirement.

### Cited Findings

- **Explicit challenge language:** build a lightweight gateway, proxy, middleware, SDK wrapper, or equivalent intermediary that intercepts and governs AI interactions, enforces security/privacy/resource controls from central configuration, and reports to security and management audiences. The layer **must** use deterministic and AI-based semantic controls; the testing suite **must** cover allowed and blocked/redacted cases. — [Brief, p. 2](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2)
- **Expected artifacts:** a functional integration point and simple architecture diagram; documented policy demonstrating different strictness/adherence levels and budget rules; simple interactive dashboard; and ready-to-run tests for controls, budget limits, and exploit mitigation. A self-built agent is optional: an existing one may demonstrate the layer. — [Brief, pp. 2–3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [Brief, p. 3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=3)
- **Formal policy requirements:** one configuration source must manage controls, sensitivity thresholds (including block versus redact or adherence percentage), allowed LLM models, and resource/financial budgets. Formal control examples include pattern matching for PII/secrets and authentication/access checks. The semantic subsection says “where possible, consider” AI-based solutions, a softer phrase than the preceding challenge section's “must implement a hybrid defense architecture”; both statements should be reflected accurately. — [Brief, pp. 2–3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [Brief, p. 3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=3)
- **Attack/budget scope:** the brief specifically calls out resource access, compute time, token spend, external commercial APIs, locally hosted models, and externally managed signatures for historical attacks. Malicious code execution, unsafe deserialization, and model-repository supply-chain exploits are illustrative examples; the brief also asks teams to review sources such as OWASP and consider further risks. — [Brief, pp. 1–3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=1); [Brief, p. 2](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [Brief, p. 3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=3)
- **Validation and weights:** judges will run the submitted tests and may send unprepared prompts, edit rules/controls/thresholds or feeds, inspect dashboards/logging, and request performance telemetry. Weights are robustness/guardrails **30%**, architecture/performance **20%**, security reporting **20%**, self-testing **15%**, and implementability/scalability **15%**. The team's requirements table records the same weights correctly. — [Brief, p. 4](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=4); [requirements.md, lines 34–48](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/requirements.md#L34>)
- **Freedom and resource limits:** no mandated language/framework, proprietary API, model, dataset, or special hardware. Open-source tooling is allowed with license review; teams must be able to build and run on their own setup without paid AI subscriptions supplied by the event. The brief names Ollama only as an example of local model hosting. — [Brief, pp. 3–4](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=3); [Brief, p. 4](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=4)
- **Team choices, not partner dictates:** synthetic analyst/outbox workflow, one TypeScript evaluation function, HTTP plus one MCP adapter, JSON policy in Supabase, and Laya/Sentinel evaluation are recommendations in the research report and technical spec. The spec explicitly calls itself a proposed contract and says the analyst client is a deterministic two-action orchestrator rather than an autonomous LLM agent. — [research-decisions.md, lines 3–22](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/research-decisions.md#L3>); [technical-spec.md, lines 1–35](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L1>)

### Inferences

- Interpret “hybrid” as two materially different detection/control paths whose outputs can affect the central policy verdict. A signature-only demo with a semantic interface that always returns `unavailable` is an honest degraded mode, but it does not demonstrate the required AI-based part to judges. This follows the challenge wording and the team's P2 plan. — [Brief, p. 2](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [research-decisions.md, lines 81–88](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/research-decisions.md#L81>)
- The brief evaluates the control layer itself, not the sophistication of a banking workflow or custom agent. A domain-neutral core with a small synthetic client is aligned, provided the controlled interaction and effect are genuine and the integration boundary is stated. — [Brief, pp. 2–3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [architecture.md, lines 7–11](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/architecture.md#L7>); [technical-spec.md, lines 5–9](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L5>)

These need team decisions and measured results; they cannot be attributed to Goldman Sachs. — [Brief, pp. 2–4](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [Brief, p. 4](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=4)

## What should the real control layer intercept and enforce, and what does “hybrid” mean in practice?

### Takeaway

The enforcement point must sit on the actual path to a model, tool, API, agent, or resource: inspect the request and context before execution, apply a policy-derived verdict, control any resulting action and returned data, then produce auditable evidence. AI-based semantic assessment supplies evidence; a trusted policy engine and code enforce identity, access, budget, redaction, and execution decisions.

### Cited Findings

- The brief's examples cover agent-to-agent, application-to-agent, agent-to-MCP, and agent-to-model communication. It identifies local access enforcement, impersonation, harmful/irreversible actions, input validation, prompt injection, output filtering, persistent memory access, and runaway loops as risks. The control layer must be able to inspect, redact, or block unsafe interactions in real time. — [Brief, pp. 1–2](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=1); [Brief, p. 2](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2)
- The project's architecture correctly states that every **managed** interaction and agent/tool action goes through enforcement, that the core should check data flow as well as prompts, and that model output is a probabilistic assessment separate from deterministic identity/limit/allowlist facts. Its proposed flow is input → normalize/context → deterministic controls → semantic controls → policy engine → verdict → execution/rejection → audit. — [architecture.md, lines 26–56](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/architecture.md#L26>)
- The current proposed demo routes `document.read` and `message.send` through one shared gate and uses an outbox record as the observable effect. It explicitly limits coverage to HTTP and its own MCP tool and disclaims interception of unrelated APIs/tools. That is an accurate integration boundary, not a general-purpose proxy. — [research-decisions.md, lines 7–20](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/research-decisions.md#L7>); [technical-spec.md, lines 5–9](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L5>)
- The proposed spec treats client-supplied actor, owner, trace, and policy versions as untrusted; it derives identity server-side, validates operation/resource/recipient, blocks an unknown operation, rechecks the send action after read, and ties policy version, budget, audit, and outbox effect into a transaction. This is a concrete enforcement design, not something the PDF prescribes in those terms. — [technical-spec.md, lines 37–41](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L37>); [technical-spec.md, lines 73–85](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L73>)
- Proposed `ALLOW/WARN/REDACT/BLOCK` precedence prevents a semantic risk score from overriding hard denial and restricts redaction to known matched spans. Policy snapshots, versioned feed, and visible detector failures make configuration changes and unavailable semantics explainable. — [technical-spec.md, lines 52–71](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L52>)

### Inferences

- Minimum credible execution sequence: authenticate the caller; normalize a typed operation and target; load one valid policy/feed snapshot; run hard access/allowlist/budget gates; inspect input and tool-result/output content; gather signatures and semantic findings; map findings through the central policy; reserve or reject budget before effects; execute only an allowed registered operation; filter the result; record verdict, policy/feed versions, measured timings, budget, and actual effect. The PDF establishes the capabilities; the team's architecture/spec supplies a plausible sequence. — [Brief, pp. 1–3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=1); [architecture.md, lines 26–56](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/architecture.md#L26>); [technical-spec.md, lines 63–79](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L63>)
- The phrase “hybrid defense” should not mean “LLM makes final allow/deny decision.” The trusted layer should combine deterministic facts (identity, authorization, signatures, budget) and semantic classifications (intent, injection, exfiltration risk), then enforce a policy verdict. This fits the PDF's paired control types and the team's explicit architectural invariant. — [Brief, pp. 2–3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [architecture.md, lines 26–39](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/architecture.md#L26>)
- To convince judges the enforcement point is real, tests should assert **side effects**, not only returned verdict strings: a blocked send creates no outbox event, an allowed send creates one, budget exhaustion stays blocked under concurrent requests, and an edited policy/feed changes a later decision. These scenarios derive from the brief's ad-hoc and configuration-modification evaluation plan and the spec's transaction model. — [Brief, p. 4](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=4); [technical-spec.md, lines 77–91](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L77>)

### Gaps

- The proposed two-action demo does not by itself exercise model allowlisting, actual model access, local compute consumption, or a true third-party agent-to-MCP/model path. These are capabilities named by the brief but outside the current action contract; a narrower claim or additional thin adapter/test is needed. — [Brief, pp. 2–3](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [technical-spec.md, lines 37–41](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L37>); [technical-spec.md, lines 65–71](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L65>)
- The draft specs leave semantic provider availability, policy on model timeout/abstention, and whether a live semantic finding changes at least one judge-visible outcome to implementation and validation. The brief gives no fallback recipe. — [Brief, pp. 2–4](/Users/julian/Downloads/e251f2e9-fc1d-45b4-b92e-03be0adce8dc.pdf#page=2); [technical-spec.md, lines 65–71](</Users/julian/Documents/ChatGPT/HackYeah 2026/docs/product/technical-spec.md#L65>)

## Where do the existing notes need correction or sharper labeling against the brief and judging criteria?

The highest-value correction is to preserve an honest fallback while making a working semantic control, real budget evidence, configurable model policy, historical exploit coverage, and measurable overhead visible when they are claimed.
