# Performance and effectiveness evidence for AI control layers (research checked 3 October 2026)

## Which benchmarks and published systems give useful evidence, and is there a best-performing system?

### Takeaway

There is no defensible universal winner. Research systems report strong results in particular agent, model, attack, policy, and scoring setups, but a low attack success rate (ASR) can also reflect broken legitimate tasks or weak attacks. The most transferable pattern for this challenge is enforcement at tool/action boundaries with a deterministic policy, while semantic components supply risk signals or sanitize untrusted content.

### Cited Findings

- The original peer-reviewed AgentDojo benchmark comprises 97 user tasks and 629 security test cases across tool-using environments, and explicitly evaluates attack and defense behavior in an extensible environment. Its original results show that agents also fail benign tasks, so security and task utility must be reported together. — [AgentDojo paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/97091a5177d8dc64b1da8bf3e1f6fb54-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf); [official repository](https://github.com/ethz-spylab/agentdojo)
- InjecAgent has 1,054 test cases for each of base and enhanced settings, spanning 17 user tools and 62 attacker tools. In its 2024 experiments, a ReAct GPT-4 agent had 24% base and 47% enhanced ASR. Those are results for the named agent and benchmark, not a current cross-system baseline. — [InjecAgent paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02691); [official repository](https://github.com/uiuc-kang-lab/InjecAgent)
- CaMeL's research artifact separates privileged agent planning from lower-trust data and enforces policy in an interpreter. Its paper reports that AgentDojo attacks on Gemini 2.5 Pro fell from 300 successful attacks to 0, while noting that some model/suite utility drops occur. The authors explicitly warn that the released research interpreter may contain bugs and may not be fully secure. — [CaMeL paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18813); [official artifact](https://github.com/google-research/camel-prompt-injection)
- Microsoft Research's Fides tracks confidentiality and integrity labels through agent actions and uses deterministic policy checks before consequential tool calls. Its paper reports stopping all AgentDojo prompt-injection attacks _with its policy checks enabled_, and reports task-completion improvements over a simpler secure planner. That result depends on the paper's threat model and labeling/policy assumptions. — [Fides paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23643); [official tutorial](https://github.com/microsoft/fides)
- A ServiceNow/Mila firewall study tested an LLM tool-output sanitizer and tool-input minimizer. On its GPT-4o AgentDojo setup, sanitizer alone reported 0% ASR with 74.09% benign utility and 69.15% utility under attack, versus 57.69% ASR, 83.02% benign utility, and 50.01% utility under attack without defense. Combined minimizer + sanitizer also reached 0% ASR but benign utility fell to 58.41%. These are that paper's benchmark results, not a promise for another agent or attack distribution. — [Firewall paper, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244)
- The same study reports sanitizer recall 99.53%, false-positive rate 0%, and precision 100% _on its AgentDojo classification evaluation_. Under a three-stage attack cascade on the Slack suite, including defense-aware refinement, sanitizer ASR instead reached 30.0%; its standard first stage was 0%. The paper itself flags weak attacks and scoring defects in existing benchmarks. — [Firewall paper, Tables 6 and 26](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244)
- AgentDyn (2026 preprint) was built to expose utility missed by short, static tasks: 60 open-ended tasks, 560 injection tests, mean trajectory 7.1 steps, with helpful third-party instructions. It reports that CaMeL had zero utility on its dynamic tasks and that PromptGuard2 left 27.15% ASR on GPT-4o. This is a separate, limited benchmark, so it does not overturn CaMeL's narrower AgentDojo result; it shows why task mix matters. — [AgentDyn paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03117); [official repository](https://github.com/leolee99/AgentDyn)
- I found no identifiable primary benchmark named **DefendBench** for AI-control-layer or prompt-injection defense. Microsoft's **DefenderBench** is a different benchmark for autonomous agents performing cybersecurity offense, defense, and knowledge tasks, and should not be cited as a guardrail effectiveness comparison. — [Microsoft DefenderBench repository](https://github.com/microsoft/DefenderBench)

### Inferences

- A HackYeah comparison should present a _security–utility frontier_ for its chosen workflow (ASR plus benign utility and utility under attack), not rank systems by ASR alone. This follows from the firewall paper's low-ASR/low-utility detector and combined-firewall rows and from AgentDyn's dynamic-task findings. — [Firewall paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244); [AgentDyn paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03117)
- Fides/CaMeL are stronger references for separating policy enforcement from model judgment; sanitizer/minimizer is a useful reference for hybrid semantic checks at tool boundaries. A hackathon implementation can borrow the boundary design without claiming those papers' guarantees or performance. — [Fides paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23643); [CaMeL paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18813); [Firewall paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244)

### Gaps

- No head-to-head study under the _same_ model, workload, hardware, threat model, policy, and current adaptive attacks establishes a universally best-performing AI control layer.
- Public benchmarks mainly study prompt injection. They do not jointly validate central policy reload, API/token/compute budgets, threat-feed updates, audit integrity, MCP and model allowlists, or end-to-end operational scalability required by this brief.

## What do measurements say about false positives, latency, and adaptive attacks?

### Takeaway

Published numbers show real costs and fragility, but they cannot predict this project's overhead. An extra semantic model call can add material latency, and a detector that looks excellent on a fixed dataset may fail a strict false-positive target or a defense-aware attack.

### Cited Findings

- On the firewall study's AgentDojo setup, total evaluation runtime for GPT-4o rose from 510.74 seconds with no defense to 1,697.19 seconds with the tool-output firewall for benign runs, and from 5,917.86 to 20,811.03 seconds with attack runs. A two-suite tool-knowledge comparison reports 3,347.93 seconds for its firewall versus 8,417.25 for CaMeL. These are _aggregate run times_ on the authors' setup, not per-request gateway latency or a general throughput benchmark. — [Firewall paper, latency tables 23–24](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244)
- OpenAI's own LLM guardrail documentation reports P50/P95 time to completion of 1,481/2,563 ms for its default GPT-4.1-mini detector and 3,994/6,654 ms for GPT-5 in its evaluation. The same page says omitting generated reasoning reduced median latency by 40% on average in its tests. These are vendor measurements of paid remote models, not local-model estimates for HackYeah. — [OpenAI Guardrails prompt-injection documentation](https://openai.github.io/openai-guardrails-python/ref/checks/prompt_injection_detection/)
- That vendor evaluation mixes 949 positive and 97 negative AgentDojo-derived samples with 537 positive internal synthetic cases. Although the reported ROC AUC for default GPT-4.1-mini is 0.987, its reported recall at a 1% false-positive rate is 0.000. High aggregate AUC or precision in this skewed set therefore should not be presented as reliable low-FPR protection for this project. — [OpenAI Guardrails benchmark description and results](https://openai.github.io/openai-guardrails-python/ref/checks/prompt_injection_detection/)
- InjecGuard's NotInject dataset contains 339 benign prompts deliberately containing attack-associated words. Its authors report that several open-source prompt guard models achieve under 60% accuracy on this benign challenge set, illustrating overblocking from keyword shortcuts. Its detector benchmark does not measure whether an actual unauthorized action would execute. — [InjecGuard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22770); [official repository](https://github.com/leolee99/InjecGuard)
- The USENIX Security 2026 paper _The Attacker Moves Second_ reports bypassing 12 recent jailbreak/prompt-injection defenses at over 90% attack success for most when attacks were adapted to each defense; most had originally reported near-zero success. This covers a diversity of defenses, not a controlled test of this future project. — [USENIX paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/nasr)
- A newer out-of-band-defense preprint reports that its reproduced Progent setup required roughly 15 times more LLM calls per task and saw Slack utility fall from 47.5% to 20.8% and Workspace utility from 56.3% to 28.5%. The paper says its own adaptive attack is limited, so these numbers are evidence of potential operating cost, not a final judgment of Progent security. — [Adaptive evaluation preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26479)
- Benchmark versions and denominators matter: the original AgentDojo publication specifies 629 security cases, whereas a later firewall study and OpenAI's guardrail documentation evaluate 949 positive cases for a particular generated/updated attack setup. Results across these versions are not directly interchangeable. — [Original AgentDojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13352); [Firewall study](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244); [OpenAI documentation](https://openai.github.io/openai-guardrails-python/ref/checks/prompt_injection_detection/)

### Inferences

- A local semantic classifier is worth benchmarking at the exact payload sizes and concurrent request rates expected in the demo; routing only ambiguous cases to a slower semantic check could reduce mean overhead, but its safety and latency gain remain unmeasured here. — [Firewall paper runtime comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244); [OpenAI Guardrails latency data](https://openai.github.io/openai-guardrails-python/ref/checks/prompt_injection_detection/)
- Deterministic policy checks for identity, permissions, tool arguments, destination, and remaining budget can enforce known constraints even when semantic detection misses an injection. Semantic assessment should be treated as evidence for the policy, with execution of side effects gated outside the model. — [Fides paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23643); [CaMeL paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18813); [USENIX adaptive-attacks paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/nasr)

### Gaps

- I found no comparable, public P50/P95/P99 and throughput data across CaMeL, Fides, the firewall study, and a local, no-paid-API hybrid control layer on identical hardware and payloads.
- Vendor detector scores generally do not establish end-to-end prevention of exfiltration, tool misuse, or excessive consumption; detector classification and executed side effects require separate measurement.

## What is a fair, reproducible evaluation plan for this brief?

### Takeaway

Run a small offline deterministic suite for every code/configuration change, and a separate end-to-end agent red-team suite when a local model is available. Report both safe outcomes and work completed, with exact versions, denominators, uncertainty, and measured overhead. The judges' spontaneous prompts and config edits make holdout and mutation tests essential.

### Cited Findings

- The firewall study defines three distinct end-to-end metrics: benign utility (task completed without attack), utility under attack (task completed despite injected content), and ASR (attacker goal achieved). It also demonstrates that ASR can be distorted by forced attack-tool use and exact-state scoring. — [Firewall study, metrics and benchmark audit](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244)
- AgentDojo publishes its benchmark environment and traces/results; InjecAgent publishes attack cases; NotInject supplies benign prompts with suspicious vocabulary. These can seed external regression cases, but each has a narrower scope than the full brief. — [AgentDojo repository](https://github.com/ethz-spylab/agentdojo); [InjecAgent repository](https://github.com/uiuc-kang-lab/InjecAgent); [NotInject/InjecGuard repository](https://github.com/leolee99/InjecGuard)
- NIST AI RMF guidance calls for rigorous software testing and performance assessment, uncertainty measures, benchmark comparisons, documentation, and testing before deployment and during operation. — [NIST AI RMF Measure function](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)
- OpenAI's official agent-evaluation guidance defines a trace as an end-to-end record of model calls, tool calls, guardrails, and handoffs; trace grading can test whether the right tool was selected or a workflow violated policy. This is a useful model for audit and scoring even if the team does not use OpenAI services. — [OpenAI agent evaluation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agent-evals)

### Inferences

- **Offline deterministic gate:** use mock semantic scores and mock tools to exercise every verdict for authorized/unauthorized identity, role, tool/model and destination, PII/secret redaction, outbound data flows, token/request/time/loop budgets, and failures of configuration or the semantic provider. Feed a new indicator and change each threshold/budget at runtime, then assert the next verdict and logged policy/feed version. This tests this brief's central-enforcement requirements without paid APIs; the side-effect checks are motivated by Fides' deterministic boundary. — [Fides policy approach](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23643); [NIST testing guidance](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)
- **End-to-end security/utility gate:** run benign, attack, and benign-with-suspicious-words cases through a local agent and mocked side-effecting tools. Score completed legitimate task, actual forbidden side effect, exfiltration sink reached, correct redaction, overblock/false-positive rate, and false-negative rate separately. Include direct and indirect injection in tool output, long payloads, multi-step tasks, and one unseen hand-written attack set. This combines AgentDojo-style outcome scoring with AgentDyn's dynamic-task critique and NotInject's overblocking cases. — [AgentDojo paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13352); [AgentDyn paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03117); [NotInject paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22770)
- **Defense-aware test:** give one teammate the published policy format and a fixed query budget to write fresh attacks against the running layer; keep these out of detector tuning and report successes and failed attempts with the budget. A fixed historical exploit corpus alone supports regression testing, not a robustness guarantee. — [USENIX adaptive-attacks paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/nasr); [Firewall cascade experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244)
- **Performance test:** on stated local hardware and software versions, pin an input mix and concurrency, warm the local model, run the same requests with control disabled, deterministic checks only, and full hybrid checks; record end-to-end P50/P95/P99, per-stage control time, throughput, memory/CPU/GPU, model tokens and estimated cost, as well as block/redaction fractions. Publish counts and repeated-run variance, and distinguish control overhead from model/tool time. Aggregate paper runtimes and remote vendor P95 values cannot replace this measurement. — [Firewall latency analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05244); [OpenAI Guardrails latency example](https://openai.github.io/openai-guardrails-python/ref/checks/prompt_injection_detection/); [NIST measurement guidance](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)
- **Audit check:** for each evaluated action, save trace ID, policy/feed version, deterministic findings, semantic score if used, final verdict, budget delta, measured latency, and whether a tool actually executed; verify logs do not expose the test secrets. This gives the outcome scorer and security dashboard a common evidence trail. — [OpenAI trace evaluation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agent-evals); [NIST documentation guidance](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)

### Gaps

- Actual effectiveness, local latency, throughput, and false-positive results cannot be supplied until this project's control layer and chosen local semantic model are implemented and measured.
- There is no off-the-shelf public benchmark in the reviewed sources that simultaneously covers the brief's budget exhaustion, live feed reload, historical code/deserialization signatures, audits, and all agent/MCP/LLM interactions; the team needs to publish its own test fixture and scoring rules.
